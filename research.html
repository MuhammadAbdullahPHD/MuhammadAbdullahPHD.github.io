Current doctoral program

Female gait biomechanics and musculoskeletal modeling

My dissertation, Gait Biomechanics in the Female Population: An AI-Informed Musculoskeletal Modeling Approach, addresses how anatomical representation and model assumptions influence biomechanical conclusions.

The work investigates sex-specific lower-limb anatomy, including pelvic and femoral geometry, and develops subject-specific and population-informed methods for musculoskeletal gait simulation. The broader aim is to reduce bias from male-based generic models and strengthen the anatomical relevance of movement analysis.

Model personalization

Subject-specific anatomy and internal loading

My modeling work examines how anatomical geometry, segment mass distribution and mechanical parameters affect predicted kinematics, joint loading and muscle forces. Current development includes statistical shape-modeling workflows for the pelvis and femur.

Related thoracolumbar studies evaluate flexible-thorax models, personalized mass distribution and thoracic disc stiffness. These investigations support a wider goal: understanding which personalization choices materially improve model interpretation.

Scalable analysis

AI-informed biomechanics workflows

I use data-driven methods alongside physics-based simulation to investigate scalable estimation of ground-reaction forces, joint loading and muscle forces. This includes evaluating ANN-predicted forces as substitutes for directly measured inputs in OpenSim workflows.

The research also considers healthy and pathological gait, with an emphasis on identifying where predictive methods remain accurate and where pathology-specific limitations emerge.

Khalifa University multimodal gait dataset

I assisted with instrumentation and synchronized treadmill-gait data collection at 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 m/s. Measurements included 19-channel EEG, Delsys surface EMG from 12 lower-limb muscles, 17 Xsens IMUs and bilateral Bertec force plates. The resulting dataset was released through PhysioNet.

Clinical gait studies

From July 2025 to July 2026, I assisted with sensor placement and data collection in studies involving adults with stroke at Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi and children with cerebral palsy at Sheikh Khalifa Medical City. Both used Xsens IMUs, Delsys lower-limb surface EMG and GAITRite measurements.

Public-communication note: Only aggregate cohort information and approved descriptions of the measurement methods are presented. No participant-level or identifiable clinical data are included.

Earlier engineering research

Solar-thermal systems and wind-resource assessment

Before specializing in biomechanics, I worked on concentrated solar thermal systems at the Masdar Institute Solar Platform. The work included commissioning and on-sun alignment of a parabolic dish, optical-performance analysis and heat-flux measurement.

My undergraduate research evaluated ERA5 and MERRA-2 reanalysis data, turbine performance and project economics for onshore and offshore wind-resource assessment in Pakistan. This earlier work established a foundation in numerical analysis, experimentation and engineering-system evaluation.
